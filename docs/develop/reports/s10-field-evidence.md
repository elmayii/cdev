# Sprint 10 — field evidence

The record behind issue #8 and its RFC: two habits the maintainer developed by hand over
three months of driving real work with CDev, mined on 2026-09-19. It is written the way the
method's own field reports are (docs 05–06): what the record shows, what it does not, and one
product, anonymized.

## Sources and their limits

- **Prompt history** — the local log of every prompt the maintainer typed, all projects,
  2026-06-06 → 2026-09-19 (1,139 prompts). The human's words only; no agent answers. Pointer:
  `H#N` = line index in that log; times are local (UTC+2).
- **Surviving transcripts** — 16 full sessions, in three projects only. Everything else was
  already gone: for any prompt before 2026-08-16, how the agent actually responded **cannot
  be checked**. Pointer: `T:<first 8 chars of the session id>`; times are UTC.
- The log and the transcripts are local and private; they are not in this repository and the
  pointers are for the maintainer's own re-verification. The load-bearing quotes below were
  re-read against the source before this file was written.
- Quotes are **translated from Spanish**. The original is kept, in italics, only for the few
  the argument rests on. Typos in the originals are the maintainer's and are left alone.

## A. The planners, and an instruction typed by hand

### What the record shows

25 prompts mention a planner skill. Removing prompts that only discuss the skill, one slip
corrected within the minute, and merging a launch with its continuation and a re-send with its
original leaves **16 distinct planner launches**. Of those:

- **10 carry a hand-written instruction to close gaps through questions.**
- **5 of the 10 spell out a per-batch, iterative protocol** — all five on the multi-repo
  planner, all from 2026-08-11 onward.
- **6 carry no such line.** Two of those six produced a whole plan with zero questions; one of
  them is the incident below. One produced two rounds of questions anyway — without the line,
  whether the planner asks is not predictable.
- The same habit predates the planner skills: six earlier prompts attach "ask me the necessary
  questions" to conditioning or sprint-analysis work (`H#264`, `H#265`, `H#540`, `H#750`,
  `H#756`, `H#838`).

On 2026-09-13 the maintainer launched a planner without the line (`H#1123`) and re-sent the
identical prompt six minutes later with one sentence appended (`H#1124`): "plan, then ask me
the questions to close gaps". The line is not decoration; its absence is corrected.

### The instruction, in the maintainer's words

> "What we are going to do now is plan each System batch, and during that planning, in each
> one, you are going to ask me how to decide the gaps that are left open — I repeat, for each
> batch." — `H#898`, 2026-08-11
>
> *"…planificar cada System batch y durante esa planificacion en cada uno me vas a preguntar
> como decidir los gaps que se vayan quedando abiertos, repito, por cada batch."*

> "…during the planning of each sprint, the gaps generated by each batch being planned are
> reviewed sequentially; those questions must be answered by me, with the suggested decisions,
> during the planning of the batch; iteratively, sprint by sprint, batch by batch (but if a
> batch generates no gap, nothing is asked — though it is almost impossible that every batch
> of a sprint generates no questions and decisions to take)." — `H#1010`, 2026-08-17
>
> *"…se revisa de forma secuencial que gaps genera cada batch que se esta planificando, esas
> preguntas deben ser contestadas por mi con las desiciones sugeridas durante la planificacion
> del batch, de forma iterativa esto se hace sprint por sprint, batch por batch (pero sin un
> batch no genera gap no se pregunta…)"*

> "As you well know, this process involves gap detection, suggestions and direct questioning
> with me. […] but assume almost nothing, be conservative […] After the grouping we will have,
> for each batch, several question steps for me to resolve. There we apply the iterative step
> of me answering you, to close each batch's gaps. And finally, everything clarified, we take
> this to our `docs/develop` structure." — `H#1038`, 2026-08-21
>
> *"…no asumas casi nada, se conservador […] tendremos por cada batch varios pasos de
> preguntas a resolver por mi […] todo aclarado, pasamos a llevar esto a nuestra estructura"*

> "Let's go batch by batch using your form feature, suggesting several answers for each
> question." — `H#1039`, 2026-08-21
>
> *"vamos batch por batch usando tu funcionalidad de formulario, sugiriendome varias
> respuestas por cada pregunta"*

> "During the creation of the two or three batches you are going to add, you will iterate
> between each batch, consulting me directly on each gap as it arises." — `H#1077`, 2026-09-01

Distilled — every element has a quote above:

| Element | What is asked for |
|---|---|
| When | During the planning of each batch and **before it is written** ("everything clarified, *then* we take this to `docs/develop`") |
| Granularity | Sequential, batch by batch — not one questionnaire |
| Conditionality | A batch that raises no gap asks nothing |
| Content | Open decisions; suggested answers with each question; a conservative stance ("assume almost nothing"). One launch adds permissions and tool connections the work will need (`H#980`) |
| Form | The host's structured question form, several suggested answers per question |
| Stop | "Everything clarified"; the human then starts execution themselves |

### The incident — 2026-08-16 → 17 (`T:aa662542`)

1. A bare launch of the multi-repo planner, no instruction attached (`H#1008`).
2. The planner edited **five plan files in about twelve minutes, asking nothing**. Decisions
   of its own went into the plan as settled — among them a security-relevant cookie setting,
   written down as a "reasoned decision".
3. The human ratified the plan and execution began; the first batch was built.
4. Next day (`H#1010`): "there are certain things we got wrong. We will accept the current
   changes, on the condition of going back for the moment and then returning to this point."
   *("hay ciertas cosas con las que procedimos mal. Vamos a aceptar los cambios actuales, con
   la condicion de retroceder por el momento…")* — followed by the per-batch parameter quoted
   above.
5. On the interactive relaunch, the planner's **own questions named what it had assumed**:
   "economic parameters I fixed without asking you" *("Parámetros económicos que fijé sin
   preguntarte")*; "the plan assumed [an open application flow] without asking you"; "my
   decision, without asking you" — about that cookie setting.

The questions existed. They arrived after the plan was written and after a batch was built
on it.

### Two further failure shapes

- **Every question up front, as prose** (`T:b0a364f6`, 2026-08-21). Despite the gap-closing
  line in the launch, the planner answered with one long message: a batch table plus a
  numbered list of questions for all eight batches. Corrected by `H#1039` (above). Eight form
  rounds followed, one per batch, and the plan was written only after the last answer.
- **Sprint-level questions instead of per-batch ones** (`T:b7334a46`, 2026-08-31). Two form
  rounds of generic questions, then every batch written at once. Twelve minutes later the
  human had to raise a batch-level gap themselves; two more questions and a re-edit of the
  plan followed. No complaint in words — the cost was the rework.

### The run that matches what is asked (`T:b7334a46`, 2026-09-01, after `H#1077`)

Ask (4 questions) 15:01 → batch written 15:14 → ask (4) 15:15 → written 15:17 → ask (4)
15:18 → written 15:22. Asking and writing interleaved, batch by batch. Nowhere in the record
does the maintainer complain of being asked too much.

## B. The three-axis status

### What the record shows

A long run of "where are we?" prompts narrows, over ten weeks, into one specific request:

- 2026-06-12 — a coverage audit against an external requirements document (single repo).
- July — "what is left of this phase?", per phase; user stories appear as the unit of testing.
- 2026-08-03 — the first recap explicitly "according to `SPRINTS.md`" (single repo).
- 2026-08-13 — a per-batch summary "for each use case / user story changed".
- **2026-08-18 — the three axes, at once** (`T:aa662542`, multi-repo workspace): "Explain what
  we have so far through the percentage of three things with respect to the sprint's total:
  1 — functional requirements; 2 — user stories (how far they get, and the ones still empty);
  3 — non-functional requirements."
- Repeated twice in the same session: "show me the current functional state the same way I
  asked you before", and a day later as a named thing — "…then give me *status-funcional*".

That is **three answered requests, in one session, over two days, on one system sprint.**
After it, surviving sessions show only generic "where did we leave off?" prompts. The habit is
real but young; the maintainer codified it retrospectively on 2026-09-19 (issue #8), adding an
order — user stories last, built over the other two — and a purpose: "this gives control over
how the sprint's objective is progressing".

### The shape that was accepted

Identical skeleton in all three answers; the second request ("the same way") is the acceptance
signal. No answer was ever corrected.

- Title → a one-paragraph **method note**.
- §1 functional requirements: heading with an approximate percentage, the previous one in
  parentheses, and "batches X of N"; a `State | Requirement` table — met / partial with a
  percentage / pending, a marker on rows that changed, pending rows naming the batch that
  will close them.
- §2 user stories: percentage and counts, then three groups — complete, partial, empty — each
  story one first-person sentence; partials carry a percentage and a reason ("API only, no
  panel — 50%"), empties carry their owning batch. No diagram: this grouping *was* the map.
- §3 non-functional requirements: the same table.
- An **executive reading**: batches closed, the three percentages, where the remaining work
  converges, live risks (a deadline, pending human gates); in the last status, a numbered
  "what is left to reach 100%".

### How those answers were produced — the part that matters

- Two of the three were produced with **zero file reads**. The third read files only to close
  a batch. All three came from the context of the session that had planned the sprint, written
  its contract and run its batches.
- The agent said so itself, in its method note: "the sprint has no formal user stories
  written — I derive them from the document's objective and the contract's acceptance
  checklist, and I declare it. The percentages are by item, with estimated partials — not an
  exact science." *("el sprint no tiene historias de usuario formales escritas — las derivo
  del objetivo del doc y del checklist beta del contrato, y lo declaro. Los % son por ítems
  con parciales estimados, no ciencia exacta.")*
- The plans checked (one workspace, three single repositories) hold a free-text objective per
  sprint and acceptance per batch. None holds a list of requirements or user stories.

So the status worked, and was accepted, as **engineering done at read time over free-form
intent** — but only ever by a reader that already held the whole sprint in conversation.
Invariant 1 says the repository is the memory and the conversation holds nothing. A status
skill's job is to make that reading work from the repository alone.

### A design the maintainer rejected, and why it is recorded here

The planner's first draft for this sprint closed that gap from the other side: have the
planner write ID'd requirement and user-story lists into each sprint, with per-batch coverage,
so that status could be computed instead of derived. The maintainer rejected it on
2026-09-19: sprints and batches already carry the intent in free form; a formalized list must
be kept current while a feature is still being discovered, decisions change mid-implementation,
and that maintenance is what drifts. In doc 07's terms the list restates the plan (it fails
the singularity test, as the workspace's state snapshot does) where field rule 4 says derive
on read. The plan's format therefore does not change, and the RFC asks for nothing on it.

## C. Not evidenced

Stated so nobody reads more into this file than is there.

- **No three-axis status was ever requested in a single repository.** `cdev-status` takes its
  shape from the system-level one by extrapolation (clarity map: PARTIAL).
- **No cold-start status was ever produced** — by anyone, in any session. The central claim of
  the status skills (it works from the repository alone) has no field evidence yet; Sprint 10's
  sandbox exercise and the maintainer's field exercise are its first tests.
- **No hallucination or drift incident in a status answer is recorded.** The maintainer's
  concern about it is prospective and is treated as such. (Adjacent, in code rather than in a
  status: on 2026-06-13 an invented requirement was caught by tracing it back to the
  specification's own item.)
- **No complaint about percentages being estimates.** They were declared as estimates and
  accepted three times.
- For every planner launch before 2026-08-16 the human's instruction is on record but the
  agent's behavior is not.
- The counts in §A come from one mining pass over a private log, by pattern and by reading;
  they were spot-checked, not independently recounted.

## D. What this supports

- **A core rule for the planners** (the RFC): close each batch's open decisions with the
  human — questions with suggested answers, batch by batch, before that batch is written; ask
  nothing where there is no gap. One recorded incident with a rollback, two further failure
  shapes with rework, one reference run, and an instruction the maintainer re-typed ten times
  in sixteen launches because the skill did not carry it.
- **Two read-only status skills** (new capability, no RFC — the loop is untouched): the
  three-axis reading that was accepted in the field, made to work from the raw chain
  sprint → batch → handoff instead of from a conversation that will not be there.
